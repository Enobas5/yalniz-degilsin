01Family

Your family may be incomplete or difficult. Your future isn't.

Growing up without a father or mother, not getting along with your family, or having no one at home to teach you things is very common. It affects you in some way, but it doesn't decide your fate.

What changes when a parent is missing?

Studies in Türkiye show that young people raised in single-parent families more often face economic, emotional and social difficulties. Research also shows that constant conflict at home seriously affects children's mental health. So what matters is not only who is at home, but how safe the home is.

According to Harvard University's Center on the Developing Child, what children who do well despite hardship most often have in common is a bond with at least one stable, supportive adult. It doesn't have to be a mother or father. It can be a teacher, a relative, a coach or a neighbour.

Why does this matter to you?

You can't bring back what is missing, but you can build other bonds in its place. And those bonds really do work. Everything in this section rests on that idea.

If your mother or father isn't with you: your rights

  • Child support. Your mother and father must share the cost of your care and education. The one you live with can sue the other on your behalf; if you are old enough to understand, you can sue too.
  • It continues after 18. If your education goes on, your parents must support you, as far as they can, until it ends.
  • Custody. After a divorce, custody is with the parent the court left you with; if one parent has passed away, with the other. If your parents aren't married, as a rule it is with your mother.
  • Visits. The parent you don't live with has the right to see you. But if this puts your peace at risk, the court can limit or end it.
  • If your father isn't in the records, you can also sue to have him recognised. Your mother's time limit is 1 year after birth; the limit set for the child was annulled by the Constitutional Court.
  • If they passed away: orphan's pension. If your mother or father paid enough premiums (usually 1800 days; for employees 5 years insured and 900 days), you may get a pension. If you don't work, until 18; in high school until 20; at university until 25. It isn't automatic; you must apply to SGK in writing.

If you can't afford a lawsuit, you can ask for a free lawyer.

You can choose your own role model

In psychology, when someone outside the family guides a young person it is called "natural mentoring". A meta-analysis of 30 studies shows that young people who have someone like this do better at school, emotionally and in behaviour. This also holds for young people growing up in hard conditions. Research shows that programmes where the young person chooses this person work too.

  1. Look around you. Which adults treat you well and earn your respect? It could be a teacher, a master of a trade, an older sister or brother figure, or someone who runs a club.
  2. Ask something small. "Could you give me some advice on this?" is enough. Most people feel honoured to be asked.
  3. Let the bond grow over time. Say thank you, share your progress and ask the next question. Mentoring doesn't happen at once, it grows slowly.

If you don't get along with your family: setting boundaries

Setting boundaries doesn't mean rejecting your family. It means knowing what you want, what you allow and what you don't, and saying it calmly and clearly. Psychologists suggest:

  • Start small. First pick the one thing that tires you most.
  • Keep it short and clear. For example: If you speak by shouting, I will leave this conversation. We can talk again when things are calm.
  • Be ready for a reaction. When a pattern that has lasted years changes, families usually object at first. That doesn't mean you did something wrong.
  • Guilt is normal. Feeling bad after setting a boundary is very common and passes with time.
  • Be consistent. You may need to repeat your boundary calmly again and again. Don't argue, just repeat.

For problems within the family, the Ministry of Family and Social Services offers free family counselling and individual counselling. It is given at Social Service Centres in all 81 provinces, and you can apply through e-Devlet (the government portal). If your family doesn't want to come, you can go alone.

If you are thinking of leaving your family

Once you turn 18, you can decide where to live. But a sudden move can leave you in a hard spot. If there is no violence, make a plan first:

  1. Gather your documents. Keep your ID, diploma and student certificate at hand. Make sure your bank account is in your name only.
  2. Housing. If you are a student, KYK state dormitories are the first option. Follow the scholarship and loan applications.
  3. Income. Before you leave, have a regular income or at least enough savings for one month's rent.
  4. Support. Social Service Centres give advice and direct you to suitable services. For financial support you can call ALO 144.

If you grew up in state care, the Preparing for Life Guide (PDF, Turkish) that the Ministry of Family and Social Services wrote for young people explains your rights on housing, education and work. Young people under state protection can stay in KYK dormitories for free.

If there is violence or neglect at home

Nothing ever justifies hitting you, starving you or frightening you. This is not a "family matter". If you are under 18, under Child Protection Law No. 5395 the state can arrange counselling, education, care, health and housing measures for you. The priority is to protect you in your own family environment where possible.

  • ALO 183: Open 24/7 and free for reporting child abuse, neglect and domestic violence. You can call for yourself or for a friend.
  • School counsellor: If you are at school, they are the first person you can talk to.
  • Sexual abuse: At Child Monitoring Centres (ÇİM) in hospitals, the statement, examination and other steps are done in one place with specialists. This way you don't have to tell what happened over and over.
  • Life in danger: 112.

If you are over 18, the rights under Law No. 6284 in the Harassment and violence section apply to you too. This law protects both children and adult family members against domestic violence. ŞÖNİM and ALO 183 help with this as well.

02Mental health

Your mind can break too. That isn't weakness.

If you broke your arm, nobody would tell you to "pull yourself together". A break doesn't heal straight by itself; you see a doctor. Depression and anxiety are the same: not a matter of willpower, but treatable health problems. Both get better. Both need care.

If you are thinking of harming yourself or ending your life

Having this thought is not weakness and nothing to feel guilty about. The World Health Organization describes it as a sign of severe emotional distress. Many people have been through it, got help and recovered.

If you are in danger right now

Call 112 now or go to the nearest emergency department. If you can't go, ask someone you trust to stay with you.

  1. Don't stay alone. Go to someone you trust or ask them to come.
  2. Tell someone. One sentence is enough: I don't feel like living, I need help. If writing is easier, send a message.
  3. Move away from anything you could hurt yourself with. Leave the place you are in or ask someone to take those things away.
  4. Put off the decision. These thoughts can feel very strong, but they can change. For now, just get through today.
  5. Tell a doctor. If it isn't an emergency, see your family doctor, book a mental health appointment through ALO 182, or talk to your school counsellor.

Source: World Health Organization, Suicide: questions and answers (2026)

If you are worried about a friend

Take these signs seriously: sudden, severe mood changes, withdrawing, remarks like "nobody would notice if I were gone", saying goodbye, giving away things they value.

  1. Ask openly. Do you feel like you don't want to live? Asking doesn't put the idea in their head; it often lowers their anxiety and makes them feel understood.
  2. Listen, don't judge. Don't say "you're exaggerating" or "you just want attention". You don't have to find a solution.
  3. Don't leave them alone, don't keep it secret. Go together to an adult or a health worker. This isn't betraying your friend.
  4. If there is danger, stay with them and call 112.
Sources (2)

World Health Organization, Suicide: questions and answers (2026) · Ministry of Health, Diyarbakır Provincial Ambulance Service (Turkish)

This is not your fault

According to the World Health Organization, about 322 million people worldwide live with depression. The risk is higher for those who lived through abuse, neglect, great loss or long-lasting stress. So if you grew up in a difficult family and are struggling today, there is a reason for it, and it isn't your fault.

Source: World Health Organization, Depression fact sheet

The difference between sadness and depression

Ordinary sadness
It is linked to an event. There are good moments in between, and it eases within a few days or weeks.
It may be depression
It lasts nearly every day for at least two weeks. You can't enjoy things you used to love. Sleep, appetite and energy change. The feeling that "nothing will get better" takes over.

Source: World Health Organization, Depression fact sheet

If you are having a panic attack

A panic attack is sudden, very intense fear with physical symptoms: racing heart, shortness of breath, shaking, sweating, dizziness, chest pain, a feeling that something terrible will happen. Most last between 5 and 20 minutes. It is very frightening, but the panic attack itself does not harm your body.

  1. Name it. Tell yourself This is a panic attack, it will pass.
  2. Slow your breathing. Breathe in calmly through your nose and out for longer through your mouth. Don't force it, and keep going for a few minutes.
  3. Afterwards, tell a doctor. If it keeps happening, see your family doctor or book the mental health department through ALO 182. It can be treated with talking therapy and, if needed, medicine.

The same symptoms can come from another health problem. If you have chest pain or shortness of breath for the first time, or it doesn't pass, call 112.

Sources (2)

NHS, Panic disorder · NHS, Breathing exercises

Exam anxiety

Constant worry, headaches and stomach aches, sleeplessness, irritability, not enjoying things you love and feeling hopeless about the future can be signs of exam stress. An exam does not measure your worth.

  • Break it into small parts. Make a realistic study plan and mix your subjects.
  • Take short, regular breaks. Studying for hours without a break ruins your focus and raises your anxiety.
  • Sleep. Most teenagers need 8–10 hours of sleep a night.
  • Ask what you don't understand. Instead of memorising, ask your teacher or a friend for help.
  • Talk. If there is pressure at home or you can't cope, tell your school counsellor. If hopelessness takes over, see this topic.
Sources (2)

NHS, Preparing for exams · NHS, Exam stress

If you feel lonely

You can feel lonely even in a crowd. It is very common, especially between 16 and 34. It isn't your fault and it can pass.

  • Send a small message. Writing "you crossed my mind" to someone you haven't talked to for a while is enough. Short, regular chats help.
  • Find a shared activity. A club, sport, course, library or volunteering. There is already something to talk about, and introducing yourself gets easier.
  • Go at your own pace. The first time, just being there is enough. If you are shy, build your own ladder.
  • Be careful with strangers online. When you're lonely, fast and intense attention feels good. Don't send money or private images to someone you haven't seen.
  • If it lasts long, tell your school counsellor or family doctor. Severe, long-lasting loneliness can raise the risk of anxiety and depression.

Source: NHS, Dealing with loneliness

If you are at war with food and your body

An eating disorder is not a phase or a matter of willpower; it is a mental health condition. It is about controlling food to cope with feelings. It can happen to anyone in any body and is most common in young people. With treatment most people recover.

  • Signs: constantly thinking about your weight and body, avoiding places with food, eating very little, eating a lot without control, making yourself sick after eating, exercising too much, periods stopping, feeling cold, dizziness.
  • What can you do? Tell someone you trust and go together to your family doctor, or book the mental health department through ALO 182. Go as early as you can.
  • If you faint, your heart races or you are very weak, don't wait; go to the emergency department or call 112.
  • If you are worried about a friend, say I'm worried about you, I'm here for you and offer to go to the doctor together. They may deny it or be defensive; this is common.

Source: NHS, Eating disorders

If you have lost someone

There is no right or wrong way to grieve. Shock, feeling nothing, crying, tiredness, anger and guilt are normal. These feelings sometimes come when you least expect them. Over time they become less intense.

  • Talk. You can talk about them with a friend, a relative or a teacher.
  • Set small goals. Don't try to do everything at once. Food, sleep and a little movement are enough.
  • Don't numb the pain with alcohol, cigarettes, drugs or gambling. They make mental health worse.
  • Get help. If you can't carry on with daily life, contact your family doctor, ALO 182 or your school counsellor. If you feel you don't want to live, look here right away.

Source: NHS, Grief and loss

What happens at the first appointment?

  1. You book an appointment at a state hospital's "Ruh Sağlığı ve Hastalıkları" (mental health) department through MHRS or by calling ALO 182.
  2. If you are covered by SGK, you only pay a co-payment for the examination: 50 TL at a state hospital, 90 TL at a training and research hospital (July 2026). Half if your family doctor refers you. Seeing your family doctor is free.
  3. The doctor asks how long you have felt this way, about your sleep and appetite, and what has been going on in your life. There is no right or wrong answer.
  4. You don't have to tell everything on the first day. The first meeting is for getting to know each other; no one expects a miracle.
  5. Medicine isn't always necessary. The doctor may suggest therapy, medicine or both. You decide together.

If you're afraid you won't be able to speak

Write three sentences on your phone beforehand, for example: I haven't been able to sleep for two months. I don't enjoy anything. There is no one at home I can talk to. You can tell the doctor "I find it hard to explain, so I wrote it down" and read it. People do this very often.

03Relationships

Love doesn't make you smaller. If it does, it isn't love.

Someone who grew up in a loveless or harsh home may take being controlled for "caring" and jealousy for "love". Knowing that what you live through has a name is the first step to seeing it.

Glossary of terms

Below are terms often met in relationships today. Each is given in English and Turkish.

Seeing one of these once doesn't make someone a bad person. What matters is whether a behaviour keeps repeating and whether it leaves you feeling afraid, guilty or worthless.

19 shown

  • Love bombing / Future fakingSevgi bombardımanı ve sahte gelecek vaadiMeeting and early days

    Winning someone over at the start of a relationship with too much attention, compliments, gifts, constant messages and plans that will never happen (a home, marriage, a holiday). The aim is not love. It is to gain control and make you feel you owe them. Put-downs usually follow.

    ExampleIn the second week: “You're my soulmate, you'll meet my family this summer, we'll move in together,” plus a hundred messages a day.

    What you can doSay “Let's slow down a bit” and watch the reaction. Real interest can wait; a love bomber gets angry or blames you. Look at actions, not words, and make a note if promises never turn into a concrete step.

    Source: Cleveland Clinic, Love bombing

  • Neggingİltifat kılıklı aşağılamaMeeting and early days

    Lowering your confidence with half-compliment, half-insult remarks so that you work to please them.

    Example“You actually look better without make-up, I'm surprised.” or “Not bad for someone like you.”

    What you can doIf you have started treating remarks that make you feel bad as jokes, stop looking for their approval. A respectful person doesn't poison a compliment.

  • CatfishingSahte kimlikMeeting and early days

    Starting a relationship online with someone else's photos or an invented identity. In the end they usually ask for money or private images.

    ExampleThe person you have been texting for months never agrees to a video call, then one day says “I need money urgently.”

    What you can doAsk for a video call and check their photos with a reverse image search. Never send money or private images to someone you have never seen.

  • Red flag / Green flagKırmızı bayrak / Yeşil bayrakMeeting and early days

    A red flag is a warning sign: extreme jealousy, every ex being “crazy”, being rude to the waiter. A green flag is a sign of safety: respecting your boundaries, being consistent, admitting mistakes.

    ExampleNot pushing when you say “no” is a green flag. Going through your phone is a red flag.

    What you can doLook at the whole picture, not a single flag. If the red ones are piling up, trust how you feel.

  • GaslightingKendi aklından şüphe ettirmeDuring the relationship

    Making you distrust your own memory and perception by denying or twisting what happened. Over time you stop trusting your own decisions.

    Example“I never said that, you exaggerate everything, you're always like this.”

    What you can doWrite down what happened with dates and keep the messages. Tell someone you trust. An outside view helps you remember what is real.

    Source: Acıbadem, Gaslighting (Turkish)

  • Emotional abusePsikolojik şiddetDuring the relationship

    Controlling someone through humiliation, threats, shouting, intimidation and constant criticism. It leaves no mark on the body, but under Law No. 6284 it is violence.

    ExampleYour stomach tightening every evening before you get home: “What will they be angry about today?”

    What you can doThis is not an argument, it is violence. Call ALO 183. No evidence or document is needed for a protective order.

    Source: Law No. 6284 (Turkish)

  • Coercive controlZorlayıcı kontrolDuring the relationship

    Step-by-step control over your money, your phone, your clothes, who you see and where you go. Rules that look small one by one add up to a cage.

    ExampleLocation sharing “to protect you”, asking for your passwords, having your salary paid into their account.

    What you can doTry to keep your financial independence and have an account in your own name. ALO 183 and ŞÖNİM (Violence Prevention and Monitoring Centres) give advice on this too.

  • Silent treatment / StonewallingSessiz ceza / Taş duvarDuring the relationship

    Not speaking for days after an argument, ignoring you, leaving questions unanswered. Psychologist John Gottman counts stonewalling among the four behaviours that wear relationships down most.

    ExampleWhen angry, they don't say a word for three days. You apologise without knowing what you did.

    What you can doTaking a break to calm down is normal. Going silent to punish is not. Ask “When can we talk?” If it keeps happening, it is a pattern.

    Source: Cleveland Clinic, Stonewalling

  • Emotional blackmailDuygusal şantajDuring the relationship

    Using fear, obligation and guilt to get you to do what they want.

    Example“If you really loved me you wouldn't go.” or “If you leave me I'll do something to myself.”

    What you can doYou are not responsible for someone else's life. If you see a serious danger call 112, but you don't have to change your decision.

  • TriangulationÜçgenlemeDuring the relationship

    Bringing in a third person to make you jealous or insecure. It can be an ex, a friend or a family member.

    Example“My ex never did that.” or “Everyone thinks you're wrong.”

    What you can doDon't join the comparison. Bring the topic back: “This is about us, nobody else.”

  • DARVOİnkâr et, saldır, rolleri değiştirDuring the relationship

    Short for “Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender”. The person who did wrong first denies it, then attacks you, and finally presents themselves as the victim and you as the guilty one.

    ExampleWhen you say “You shouted at me”: “Me? You're the one who makes me like this, you're toxic.”

    What you can doTake the conversation back to the start: “Right now we're talking about you shouting.” If the cycle doesn't change, the problem is not how you explain it. It is the other person.

    Source: WomensLaw, DARVO

  • Trauma bondingTravma bağıDuring the relationship

    Feeling strongly attached to someone who hurts you, in a cycle where good and bad days alternate. It is often the answer to “Why can't I leave?” Its roots can lie in the bond with caregivers in childhood.

    ExampleA fight, then an apology and a lovely week, then another fight. Every “good day” ties you a little more.

    What you can doThis is not weakness. It is how the brain reacts to a cycle of reward and punishment. Cut contact, read your notes and get support from a therapist.

    Sources (2)

    Cleveland Clinic, Trauma bonding · Psychology Today, Trauma bonding

  • Breadcrumbing / BenchingOyalama: ekmek kırıntısı ve yedek kulübesiDuring the relationship

    Giving just enough attention to keep you, never enough to be clear (breadcrumbing), or keeping you on the side as a plan B (benching).

    ExampleWeeks of silence, then just as you are giving up, a “what's up” at midnight. Plans are always cancelled at the last minute.

    What you can doAsk yourself: “What do I want from this relationship, and what am I getting?” If the answers don't match, ask for clarity or let go. Don't spend your time waiting where you are not a priority.

  • SituationshipAdı konmamış ilişkiDuring the relationship

    A relationship that is neither friendship nor dating, with unclear limits and no clear future. If both people want it that way, it is fine. The problem is when one wants more and the uncertainty goes on.

    ExampleYou talk every day, but the subject changes whenever you ask “what are we?”

    What you can doSay clearly what you want. If the answer stays vague, that is an answer too.

  • Ghosting / Orbiting / ZombieingHayalet gibi kaybolma ve geri dönmeBreak-up and after

    Cutting off contact with no explanation (ghosting). Sometimes the person doesn't write but keeps watching your stories (orbiting), or comes back months later as if nothing happened (zombieing). Research shows that being watched from a distance is more confusing than being rejected outright.

    ExampleYou had a nice chat last night. Today your messages are seen but not answered. Six months later: “Hey stranger, you crossed my mind.”

    What you can doDisappearing shows how they communicate, not what you are worth. Hide your stories from them or block them. You don't have to accept a return without an explanation and an apology; not replying is also a reply.

    Source: University of Colorado, Ghosting and orbiting

  • HooveringGeri çekmeBreak-up and after

    Named after a vacuum cleaner brand. Trying to pull you back after a break-up with promises, compliments, gifts or a manufactured crisis.

    Example“I've changed, I started therapy, I can't live without you.” Then everything goes back to how it was.

    What you can doChange is proven by consistent behaviour over a long time, not by words. Look at your notes about the earlier cycles.

    Source: Cleveland Clinic, Hoovering

  • SextortionCinsel içerikli şantajDigital

    Being threatened with a private photo or video to get money, new images or something else. Under the Turkish Penal Code this is blackmail (article 107) and it is prosecuted even without a complaint.

How do you get out of these and heal?

  1. Name it. Seeing that what you live through is a pattern breaks the "it's my fault" thought. Write down what happened with dates, in a notebook or on your phone. When you read it, the fog clears.
  2. Cut or reduce contact. When you feel safe, block their number and social media accounts, change your passwords and turn off location sharing. Every message can restart the old cycle.
  3. If you have to see them, be a "grey rock". If you must meet because of work, school or a child, keep your answers short, calm and unemotional: "OK", "Thursday works". Manipulation weakens when it gets no reaction.
  4. Missing them is normal, going back isn't required. With a trauma bond, the longing after a break-up is as intense as withdrawal. It shows how strong the cycle was, not that the relationship was good. When a wave of longing comes, read your notes.
  5. Rebuild your circle. Writing to the friends you drifted from is enough: "I disappeared for a while, I'm sorry, I miss you." Most people understand.
  6. Get support. If you keep ending up in similar relationships, the root is often the bond with caregivers in childhood. You can work through this pattern with a therapist. Places to go are in the Mental health section.

If you were harassed or sexually assaulted

You are not to blame. What you wore, why you were there, having drunk alcohol, freezing or not being able to shout doesn't change that. Having said yes before or being in a relationship isn't consent either. Most people who do this are not strangers; they are someone known, such as a partner, relative, friend or colleague. It can happen to anyone of any age, girls and boys.

If you are in danger right now

Get to a safe place and call 112. If you are in the street or on public transport, call loudly for help and move towards an official or a crowd.

  1. Don't stay alone. Tell someone you trust and ask them to be with you.
  2. After a sexual assault, go to a hospital. Even if you don't plan to file a complaint, you need to be examined for injuries, infection and pregnancy. For infection the first 72 hours matter, for pregnancy the morning-after pill; the sooner you go, the better.
  3. Know about reporting beforehand. Health workers must report to the authorities if they see signs of a crime. This isn't to punish you; you can ask them what rights you have in the process.
  4. If possible, don't wash or change your clothes. Traces collected in the examination can be evidence. If you have washed, it isn't too late; you can still report it. Having no marks on your body doesn't mean there was no assault.
  5. Keep the evidence. Don't delete messages or call logs; take screenshots. Write down the place, the time and what you saw, as far as you remember. You can ask for nearby cameras to be checked.
  6. Report it. You can go to the police, the gendarmerie or the prosecutor; if you go to a hospital, the police become involved from there. If you don't know what to do, ALO 183 will guide you.
  7. Give yourself time. Fear, sleeplessness, shame or feeling nothing at all are common. For support see the Mental health section.

Your rights after harassment and sexual assault

  • Harassment can happen without touching (Penal Code 105). Sexually motivated acts such as catcalling, persistent sexual messages or sending unwanted images can be the crime of sexual harassment. It depends on a complaint.
  • Penalty for sexual harassment: 3 months–2 years in prison or a judicial fine; if the victim is a child, 6 months–3 years in prison. It is increased by half if done within the family, at work, by a teacher or carer, or by message.
  • Unwanted sexual touching is sexual assault (Penal Code 102). The penalty is 5–10 years in prison, or 2–5 years if it stays at the level of a sudden, brief touch; it depends on a complaint. For rape the penalty is at least 12 years and no complaint is needed. It is also a crime if a spouse does it; in that case your complaint is needed.
  • Sexual abuse of anyone under 18 is a crime (Penal Code 103). Any sexual act towards someone who has not turned 15 is abuse; consent is not valid. Between 15 and 18, acts done by force, threat, deceit or another reason that affects the will are abuse.
  • If a family member did it, the law treats it as more serious. You don't have to protect anyone's reputation. ALO 183 and the Child Monitoring Centres in the Family section are there for this.
  • You can ask for a free lawyer. For sexual assault, sexual abuse of a child and stalking, if you have no lawyer you can ask the bar association to appoint one. If you are under 18 you don't even need to ask.
  • You don't have to tell it over and over. If the event has affected you psychologically, as a rule you are heard as a witness once and a specialist is present. For rape, and the same severe form of abuse of a child, your statement is taken at centres that work with specialists.
  • Protection and privacy. If you are a woman or a child, if the person is a family member, or if you are being stalked, you can ask under Law No. 6284 for an order that they stay away from you. If needed, your address and identity are kept confidential in official records.
  • If it happens at work, report it in writing. If the employer harasses you, or takes no measures although you reported someone else's harassment, you can end your contract immediately with just cause.
  • Time limit. For crimes that depend on a complaint, file it within 6 months of learning of the act and who did it (Penal Code 73). Even if the time has passed, ask a lawyer; not every case is the same.

The penalties given are for adults; the rules differ if the person who did it is under 18 (Penal Code 31).

If they want to marry you off by force or too young

You decide whether, whom and when you marry. Tradition, debt or a "family promise" doesn't change this.

  • There is an age limit. No one under 17 can marry. For someone who has turned 16, only a judge can give permission, in exceptional circumstances and for a very important reason. Under 18 your guardian's permission is also needed, but their wish does not replace yours.
  • With someone who has not turned 15, any sexual act is abuse (Penal Code 103). Holding a wedding or a religious ceremony doesn't change this.
  • If you were married through fear, you can sue to annul the marriage. You must have been frightened with a close and serious danger to your life, health or honour. The limit is 6 months after the fear ends, and 5 years after the marriage in any case.
  • Threatening someone and holding them somewhere by force are crimes (Penal Code 106 and 109).

  2. Call. ALO 183 is open 24/7 and free. If the danger is close or they are taking you somewhere, 112.
  3. Ask for protection. You can ask for protection and shelter through the police, the prosecutor or ŞÖNİM. If you are under 18, measures under Child Protection Law No. 5395 can also apply.
  4. Keep your ID with you. If it is safe, keep your ID, your phone and a trusted person's number at hand.

Know your legal rights

  • Psychological violence is violence too. Law No. 6284 covers physical, sexual, psychological, verbal and economic violence. It protects women, children, family members and anyone who is stalked; being at risk of violence is enough.
  • How do you ask for protection? You can apply to the police, the prosecutor, the district or provincial governor, the family court or ŞÖNİM. No evidence or document is needed for protective measures; preventive measures such as removal from the home are also given without delay (article 8).
  • Stalking is a crime (Penal Code 123/A). Persistently following someone, or trying to make contact by phone, message, online or through others, is a crime if it causes you serious distress or fear for your safety. The penalty is 6 months–2 years in prison; 1–3 years if the victim is a child, or a spouse under a separation decision or divorced. You don't need to be related. It depends on a complaint.
  • Sharing your private images is a crime (Penal Code 134/2). Even if the image was taken or sent with your consent, showing or spreading it to others without your consent is a crime. The penalty is 2–5 years in prison. It depends on a complaint. Obscene images of anyone under 18 also fall under Penal Code 226; there the child's consent doesn't matter and no complaint is needed.
  • Be careful when gathering evidence. Possessing, copying or sending an obscene image of anyone under 18 is a crime (Penal Code 226); even if the image is of you, don't copy it or send it to anyone. The link, the username and screenshots of the chat are enough; the police or prosecutor examines the image.
  • Blackmail with images is a crime (Penal Code 107). Threatening to publish your images to gain something carries 1–3 years in prison and a judicial fine. It doesn't depend on a complaint, so the prosecutor can start an investigation on their own.
  • Don't miss the deadline. For crimes that depend on a complaint (such as 123/A and 134), you must file it within 6 months of learning of the act and who did it (Penal Code 73).
  • If the offender is under 18 the penalties differ (Penal Code 31). The penalties above are for adults.

Your body is changing: is this normal?

Most likely yes. Puberty can start any time between the ages of 8 and 14. Starting before or after your friends is normal.

  • Everyone: hair under the arms and around the genitals, a growth spurt, more sweating, acne.
  • Girls: breasts grow, hips widen, periods start.
  • Boys: the voice cracks and deepens, the penis and testicles grow, facial hair appears. Ejaculating during sleep (a wet dream) can happen; it is normal and nothing to be ashamed of.
  • Sweat and smell: wash your armpits, groin and feet with soap and dry well, use deodorant, and change underwear and socks often.
  • Acne is very common and treatable. If it hurts, leaves marks or upsets you, see your family doctor or a dermatologist.
  • See a doctor: if signs of puberty started before age 8, or there are none by age 15. Usually it's nothing to worry about.

Your first period and after

A period is not an illness or something dirty. For most it starts around age 12; starting earlier or later usually isn't a problem.

  • How long does it last? It usually comes every 21 to 35 days and lasts 2 to 7 days. It can be irregular in the first years and mostly becomes regular by 16–18.
  • What can I use? A pad, tampon, menstrual cup or period underwear. A pad is easiest: stick the adhesive side to your underwear. A tampon can't get lost inside you.
  • If you don't have a pad at school, tell the counsellor, a female teacher or a friend. They all know about it.
  • For pain, a hot water bottle (wrapped in a towel), massaging your tummy and gentle movement can help. Ask a pharmacist about painkillers.
  • See a doctor: if painkillers don't help or pain stops your daily life, if bleeding gets much heavier, or if you bleed between periods.

What is consent?

Consent means saying "yes" willingly and freely. Not saying "no" doesn't mean you said yes.

  • Not consent: staying silent, freezing, agreeing out of fear or because you feel you have to, anything done while you're drunk or asleep.
  • It can be withdrawn. Even if you said yes at first, you can change your mind at any moment.
  • It is asked every time. Having said yes before, or being a couple, engaged or married, doesn't replace consent.
  • You ask too. If you're not sure, stop and ask: Do you want this? If the answer isn't a clear yes, don't go on.
  • Age: in law the consent of someone who has not turned 15 isn't valid. For ages 15–18 see this topic.

How does it not spread?

  • HIV does not spread by kissing, hugging, shaking hands, or sharing the same items, food or water. Saliva, sweat, coughing, shared toilets and insect bites don't spread it either.
  • Hepatitis C does not spread by food, water, hugging, kissing or eating from the same plate; it spreads through blood.
  • Careful: Hepatitis A can spread through dirty water and food and close contact. Hepatitis B can also spread through saliva if there is an open wound. Both have a vaccine.
  • Herpes can spread by kissing.

Protection: what does each one do?

  • Condom. Use it every time, from start to finish, for vaginal, anal and oral sex. It is the most reliable protection against HIV. But it protects only partly against infections that spread by skin contact, such as syphilis, herpes and HPV, because skin not covered by the condom can also pass them on.
  • Lubricant. Use only water-based ones. Petroleum jelly, baby oil and lotion weaken the condom and make it tear.
  • Vaccines. Hepatitis B and hepatitis A vaccines are free in the childhood schedule. Ask your family doctor whether you had them. The HPV vaccine is not in the national schedule; it is bought at pharmacies (as of November 2026).
  • Personal items. Don't share razors, toothbrushes, nail clippers, needles or manicure sets with anyone. Get tattoos and piercings only where single-use needles are used.
  • While you have symptoms. If you have a sore, blister, discharge or itching, don't have sex until treatment is finished.
  • Don't stop treatment halfway. Finish the medicine even if symptoms go. Your partner must be treated at the same time, or the infection goes back and forth between you.
  • Regular testing. When you have a new partner or after a risky situation, get tested without waiting for symptoms.

Preventing pregnancy and the morning-after pill

  • Condoms are the only method that protects against both pregnancy and infections. Used correctly every time, they prevent pregnancy about 98% of the time.
  • Withdrawal and calendar methods are less reliable. Ask a doctor about the pill, injection, implant and IUD; these don't protect against infections.
  • After unprotected sex, hurry. The sooner the morning-after pill is taken, the better it works. The World Health Organization recommends emergency contraception within the first 5 days; the product information of levonorgestrel pills in Türkiye gives 72 hours. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which medicine suits you and whether you need a prescription.
  • The morning-after pill prevents or delays ovulation; it does not end a pregnancy that has started. It doesn't protect against infections and isn't a regular method.
  • If your period is late, take a pregnancy test and see your family doctor or a gynaecology department. Knowing early gives you more options.
  • The law on unwanted pregnancy: In Türkiye a pregnancy can be ended at the woman's request until the end of the 10th week if there is no medical risk. Under 18, her own consent and a guardian's permission are needed; for married women, the spouse's consent. In a life-threatening emergency permission isn't required. If the pregnancy resulted from a crime, the limit is 20 weeks and it must be done by a specialist in a hospital.

06Addiction

There is no drug that "won't do anything".

The most dangerous sentences about drugs are "once won't hurt", "it's natural, it's harmless" and "I can control it". This section uses official sources to explain why they are wrong. If you have started, the road hasn't ended: treatment is free, and if you apply on your own before an investigation starts, you are not punished for using. How does addiction start?

Yeşilay (Green Crescent) Counselling Centre (YEDAM) defines drug addiction as being unable to stop a substance despite the harm. The person loses control over use, has withdrawal when trying to quit, and over time the addiction becomes both physical and psychological.

Nobody starts by saying "I'll become an addict". It starts with curiosity, a friend's pressure, trying "just once" at a party, or to forget a trouble. According to Yeşilay, use can become addictive once it starts to repeat. The Ministry of Health says some substances can cause addiction even with a single use, and that no one can know in advance who will become addicted and when.

Why is it more dangerous for young people?

The brain is still developing in adolescence. According to the World Health Organization, even nicotine harms brain development in children and adolescents, and cannabis impairs learning.

The lies you hear and the truth

"It's natural, a plant, no harm."
Cannabis comes from a plant too; according to the World Health Organization it is addictive and impairs learning. Bonzai isn't even natural: it is a lab chemical added to dried herbs. Bonzai was found in 63% of those who lost their lives to drugs in Türkiye in 2025.
"Once won't hurt."
Inhalants such as thinner and glue can stop the heart even in people who use them only once. With cocaine, sudden loss of life is possible, though rare, even on first use.
"I know what I'm taking."
Pills sold as "ecstasy" can turn out to be methamphetamine and ketamine. Many people who test positive for fentanyl don't know they took it. Even the seller may not know what is in a street drug.
"I can control it, I can stop whenever I want."
Addiction is exactly the loss of that control. According to Yeşilay, addiction can be treated, but the treatment isn't easy. The easiest way is never to start.
"Everyone uses."
They don't. According to United Nations data, 6% of people aged 15–64 worldwide used drugs in 2023 (not counting alcohol and tobacco). So 94% didn't.
"Alcohol and e-cigarettes are innocent."
According to the World Health Organization there is no safe amount of alcohol that does not affect health. Nicotine is highly addictive, and among young non-smokers e-cigarettes make starting regular cigarettes about 3 times more likely.

If a friend gets worse: overdose

Overdose can look different with each substance. If you see any of these, call 112 without waiting:

  • Unconscious, you can't wake them.
  • Struggling to breathe, breathing very slowly or stopping.
  • Pupils shrunk to pinpoints (a sign of opioids).
  • Body very hot, heart beating very fast or irregularly, having a seizure.
  1. Call 112 and do what the operator says.
  2. Don't leave them alone. If they are breathing but unconscious, lay them on their side so they don't choke if they vomit.
  3. If you know what they took, don't hide it. Telling the medical team can save a life.

If you want to quit: step by step

  1. Tell one person or call. ALO 191 is open 24/7 and free from landlines. They inform you and direct you to the most suitable treatment centre; you can call even if you are undecided. Yeşilay's 115 line is also free.
  2. Get free psychological support. YEDAM gives completely free outpatient support to people aged 12 and over with drug addiction. A psychologist meets you for an assessment and you make a treatment plan together.
  3. If needed, apply to a treatment centre. AMATEM is for adults, ÇEMATEM for children and adolescents. Outpatient treatment is also possible. You can book through ALO 191 or ALO 182.
  4. How long does treatment take and what does it cost? According to YEDAM, inpatient treatment includes 2–6 weeks of detox followed by a year of psychosocial support; the length is planned for each person. No insurance premium days are required and premium debt doesn't matter for addiction treatment; it is free. In 2024, 373 thousand people applied to AMATEM centres; you are not alone.
  5. Change your surroundings. Delete the numbers of people you used with and don't go to those places. In the first weeks after quitting, stay away from places that trigger you.
  6. If you slip, don't give up. According to Yeşilay, addiction can be treated, but the treatment isn't easy. One slip doesn't mean everything is over; tell your specialist and carry on.

Asking for treatment doesn't make you a criminal

  • No penalty if you apply before an investigation starts. Under article 192(4) of the Turkish Penal Code, a person who applies to official authorities or health institutions and asks for treatment before an investigation for using starts is not punished. In this case doctors and public officials also have no duty to report you.
  • You must apply of your own will: going because of a treatment or probation decision already made about you doesn't count.
  • If you are caught, treatment is still open. The penalty for possession for personal use is 2–5 years in prison (Penal Code 191). But the case is postponed for 5 years, at least 1 year of probation applies, and you get treatment if needed.
  • If you follow the rules, no case is opened. If you keep breaking them, or use, buy or possess again, a case is opened and there is no second postponement for a later offence.
  • Giving it to a friend can also be trafficking. Giving or selling to someone else is drug trafficking under Penal Code 188/3, with at least 10 years in prison; at least 15 years if the person is a child. According to the Court of Cassation, taking no money doesn't change this.

The penalties given are for adults; separate rules apply to those under 18 (Penal Code 31). If you have a legal problem, you can apply for the bar association's free legal aid in the Your rights section.

Gambling and online betting

Like drugs and alcohol, gambling addiction affects the brain's reward system. The World Health Organization defines gambling disorder as a health condition. Thinking "one last time, let me win back what I lost" is one of its signs.

  • Signs: betting more and more money, chasing losses, trying to stop and failing, lying to hide it, borrowing money, neglecting school or work.
  • Illegal betting has penalties too. Players get an administrative fine. Advertising it carries 1–3 years in prison, and helping move betting money 3–5 years. "Renting out" your account can make you part of this crime.
  1. Make it harder to reach. Delete the apps, block the sites, unfollow betting accounts and groups.
  2. Move money out of reach. Carry only what you need for the day. Leave your cards with someone you trust for a while.
  3. Tell someone. However big the debt is, hiding it makes it grow.
  4. Get free support. YEDAM gives free psychological support for gambling too, from age 12: 115. If you feel you don't want to live because of debt, 112.

Games, phone and social media

Not everyone who plays a lot is addicted. In the World Health Organization's definition the measure is not time but losing control: gaming becoming more important than anything else, being unable to stop despite harm, and school, work and relationships breaking down. This is usually expected to last at least 12 months.

  • Ask yourself: Do I try to stop and fail? Am I giving up sleep, schoolwork or friends? Do I open it to escape a problem?
  • Start small. Turn off notifications, leave your phone in another room at night, and plan something for the free time.
  • Get free support. YEDAM also supports people with internet addiction: 115. You can tell your school counsellor too.

If speaking is hard for you, there is a mechanism behind it.

Why does avoidance make anxiety grow?

  1. A setting feels scary
  2. You avoid it
  3. You feel relief
  4. Your brain says "good thing we left"
  5. Next time the fear is bigger

Avoiding brings relief in the short term but teaches the brain that the situation really was dangerous. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), the most used method for social anxiety, focuses on breaking this cycle. You face feared situations at your own pace and in small steps. Experts call it "graded exposure".

Source: Akkuş, Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Social Anxiety Disorder (Mediterranean Journal of Humanities, 2024, Turkish)

You build your own ladder

Nobody is telling you to start with the hardest step. Find something that is 2 or 3 out of 10 for you and start there.

Example ladder · the number on the right is the difficulty

  1. Reacting to a message with just an emoji in a class or work WhatsApp group2/10
  2. Writing a one-word message in the group, like "thanks"3/10
  3. At a pharmacy or shop, showing a written note instead of asking aloud, then one day asking aloud5/10
  4. A short face-to-face chat with one person you know6/10
  5. Staying in a small group for 10 minutes8/10

If a step feels too hard, add a smaller one in between. Taking a step back isn't failure.

Source: NHS, Facing your fears

What happens in your body when you're anxious?

A racing heart, blushing, shaking, sweating and a weak voice are reactions of your body's alarm system. They are unpleasant but not dangerous. If you don't run from the situation, anxiety falls by itself after a while. Breathe in through your nose for four seconds and out through your mouth for six. A long out-breath calms the body.

And know this: people always overestimate how much others notice them. In psychology this is called the "spotlight effect". Far fewer people notice you blushing than you think.

Source: Acıbadem, Social phobia (Turkish)

Ready-made sentences for hard moments

If anxiety gets in the way of school, work or relationships, you don't have to deal with it alone. Social anxiety can be treated. If face-to-face therapy feels hard, an online session can be a start.

Free lawyer: legal aid

If you can't afford a lawyer, you can apply to the Legal Aid Office of the bar association in your province. If your application is accepted, a lawyer is appointed and the bar pays the lawyer's fee.   2. Go to the bar's legal aid office and fill in the application form.
  3. If your application is refused, you can object to the bar president within 10 days of being notified.

If you were scammed online

  1. Call your bank at once. Use the number on the back of your card or in the bank's own app. Report the transaction, have the card and account blocked, and change your passwords.
  2. Keep the evidence. Take screenshots of receipts, the IBAN, messages, the ad and the profile.
  3. File a complaint. Go to the police or the Chief Public Prosecutor's Office. Fraud using the internet or banks is a crime with a heavier penalty (Penal Code 158).
  4. Don't pay anyone who says "I'll get your money back". This is often a second trap.
  • Never share an SMS code or password. Even if they say they are the bank, the police or a prosecutor, hang up and call the institution yourself.
  • Don't let anyone use your account. An offer like "we'll rent your IBAN for monthly money" can make you part of a crime. The police warn about this in particular.
